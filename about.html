ABOUT THE WEBSITE

Build confidence for the moment a suspicious message arrives.

Shield-Up is an interactive cybersecurity learning experience that helps people recognize phishing and practice the first steps of responding to a compromised account.

Instead of only reading a list of warnings, you work through a simulated school-account incident. You inspect a suspicious message, assess its impact, make timed response decisions, and finish with habits that can help prevent another attack.

Why Shield-Up exists

Phishing messages often use urgency, familiar-looking services, and links to imitation sign-in pages to persuade someone to reveal a password. Shield-Up turns those warning signs into a guided exercise, so learners can notice them and think through what to do before facing a real incident.

The goal is practical awareness: pause before clicking, verify where a message came from, protect accounts with multi-factor authentication, and report suspicious activity to the right people.

What you can practice

  1. IdentifyInspect the sender, greeting, wording, threat, and link to spot signs of phishing.
  2. AssessConsider what may be at risk after a password is entered and an unfamiliar login appears.
  3. RespondChoose actions for a possible account compromise and see immediate feedback on each decision.
  4. PreventReview habits such as using unique passwords, enabling MFA, and reporting suspicious messages.

A safe, simulated exercise

The scenario is fictional. Shield-Up does not send messages, collect credentials, connect to school accounts, scan devices, or contact an IT team. The score and timeline reflect choices made in the exercise; they are learning feedback, not a real security assessment.

If you think you have encountered a real phishing attempt or account compromise, stop interacting with the message and contact your school or organization’s IT/security support using a trusted channel.

Frequently asked questions

What is Shield-Up?

Shield-Up is a browser-based training website about recognizing phishing and responding to a simulated account compromise. It guides you through identifying warning signs, assessing risk, choosing response steps, and reviewing prevention habits.

Is the phishing message real?

No. The message, school account, and incident are part of a fictional scenario created for practice. The page does not send or receive email.

Does Shield-Up ask for or store my password?

No. Never enter a real password into a training scenario or a link from a suspicious message. Shield-Up does not connect to accounts or collect login credentials.

How does the score work?

The score changes in response to choices inside the exercise, such as finding message warning signs or selecting a response. It is only a game-like learning aid and does not measure your device’s security or predict whether an account is safe.

Why are some decisions timed?

The response scenarios use a short timer to add pressure and encourage quick practice. A timeout affects the exercise score only; it has no effect on a real account or device.

What should I do if I entered my password on a suspicious site?

Use a trusted device and the official service address to change the password, enable multi-factor authentication if available, and sign out other sessions. Report the incident promptly to your school or organization’s IT/security team. If you reused that password elsewhere, change it there too.

Who is Shield-Up for?

The example is framed around a school account, but the warning signs and prevention habits can help anyone who uses email, messaging, or online accounts. It is an educational introduction, not a replacement for expert security training.
